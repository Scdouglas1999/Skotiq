# Skotiq 4.3.0

4.0 put your rig on the couch; 4.1 made it solid; 4.2 hardened the sequencer.
4.3 is the **science release**: it takes the scientific-data-collection features —
photometry, light curves, period/transit search, AAVSO/MPC export — that were
already real but buried, and turns them into a first-class part of the app, with
a path that starts at "watch your stars get measured" and leads all the way to
"submit to AAVSO." It also deepens the underlying astrometry.

## What's verified

Skotiq is verified on **Windows**, where the desktop app drives ASCOM/Alpaca
cameras, focuser, filter wheel, and PHD2, with plate-solving and planning. Remote
**monitoring and planning** over the LAN — from the web dashboard and the Android
companion — are verified. Fully-unattended **headless** acquisition on real ASCOM
hardware is **still being hardened** and should be supervised. **Linux** is in
active testing (it's the development workstation for this release); **macOS**
builds in CI with no hardware soak. Every other device path is **capability-gated**.

The 4.3 science and astrometry changes are verified at **static-analysis +
unit/widget-tested** level (the core suite runs ~3,600 tests, including the new
projection and persistence coverage), on top of the 4.1 real-hardware baseline.
They were **not** re-run on a physical rig or a real photometry session. See the
on-sky caveat at the bottom — it matters more this release than ever.

## Science is now a place you go

Science used to be the fifth sub-tab of Analytics, with the live tools hidden
behind an "Advanced Science Mode" switch most people never found.

- **A top-level Science destination.** Photometry, light curves, the field-quality
  maps, the night story, and exports now live at their own `/science` home, in the
  navigation rail and the mobile tab bar. The old `/analytics?tab=science` link
  still works.
- **A five-step on-ramp.** A guided ladder — *see your stars measured → track a
  target → build a light curve → find a period or transit → contribute to
  AAVSO/MPC* — meets you wherever your data is and points at the next real action,
  so the serious tools stop being a wall and start being a path.
- **The live tools are no longer gated.** The Science HUD, the per-frame
  zero-point / limiting-magnitude / star-count readout, and the photometry anchors
  are reachable without flipping a hidden switch; only the advanced overlay *layers*
  sit behind a clearly-named preference now.
- **Observing alerts feed the science.** The transient/variable-star alert feed is
  now a tab inside Science — it's a citizen-science observing source, so that's
  where it belongs.

## Navigation, consolidated

The mobile bottom bar had grown to a long scrolling strip where most destinations
were hidden. It's now a fixed set of six — Dashboard, Equipment, Imaging,
Sequencer, Science, Plan Tonight — and the rest fold into the screens they belong
to instead of competing for a slot:

- **Framing and Planetarium** became a **"Sky"** tab inside Plan Tonight.
- **Flat Wizard** is a launcher from Imaging/Equipment (like Polar Alignment).
- **Weather** is reached from the dashboard weather card; **Guiding** from its
  Imaging tab.

Every old route still resolves — folded ones redirect, so notifications and
bookmarks keep working.

## Photometry and astrometry, deeper

- **Measured FWHM is persisted.** The star-detection FWHM the core already computed
  is now written per frame and used directly by the auto-grader's FWHM rule instead
  of being re-derived, so frame grading reflects the real measurement.
- **Real distortion in the plate solution.** Plate solving no longer collapses the
  WCS to a single isotropic scale + rotation: it now carries the full anisotropic
  **CD matrix** and parses **SIP** polynomial distortion, and the projection applies
  them — anisotropic scale/skew plus polynomial field distortion — when correlating
  catalog stars and overlays. This is threaded through the live photometry path, the
  stored-frame path, and post-session masters; it falls back to the old isotropic
  math when a solver reports no distortion, so nothing regresses.

## Polish and consistency

- **Period analysis** got the most attention: the min/max-period fields no longer
  lose your cursor mid-edit (they rebuilt their controller every keystroke), the
  Lomb-Scargle / BLS results now carry plain-language interpretation (what a given
  false-alarm probability or BLS SDE actually means), axes are labeled with units,
  and the panel reflows instead of overflowing on a phone.
- **The photometric-calibration wizard** now actually shows the match-quality and
  extinction-fittability feedback it was already computing (it was being discarded),
  can search standard-star fields across sessions, and draws its step labels.
- A consistency sweep moved the new and touched science surfaces onto the shared
  design system — dialogs, sliders, chips, empty states, tap targets, and spacing —
  and removed a duplicated zero-point readout on the live imaging view.

## Storage

Two additive migrations: schema **v50** persists per-frame FWHM, and **v51** stores
the solved CD matrix and SIP coefficients alongside the existing WCS so the
distortion-aware projection works on stored frames too. Both add columns guardedly
and carry synthetic regression coverage; verifying them against a real older
database remains an owner checklist item.

## The on-sky caveat (read this before submitting measurements)

The photometry and astrometry work in 4.3 is tested against synthetic data and is
internally consistent — the new CD/SIP projection round-trips to sub-pixel accuracy
in unit tests and reduces exactly to the old isotropic result when there's no
distortion — but its accuracy on **real distorted optics has not been validated
against on-sky frames**, because that requires a real night and a field with
published comparison-star magnitudes. The distortion correction can only be proven
to *help* with real data, not synthetic fixtures. Shoot a known field before you
submit anything to AAVSO or the MPC. Fully-unattended headless runs should still be
supervised; iOS and live cellular-push delivery still need a Mac/Apple Developer
account and your own Firebase/APNs keys.
