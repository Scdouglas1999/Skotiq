# Skotiq 5.0.0 — Living Sky

4.0 put your rig on the couch; 4.1 made it solid; 4.2 hardened the sequencer; 4.3
made science a place you go. **5.0 is the Living Sky release.** It does two big
things at once: it turns every frame you shoot into part of a sky that grows and
remembers — your own atlas, your own discoveries, a shared community swarm — and
it lets you **monitor and control your rig from a second machine with broad
state parity**. Underneath both, the whole "automatic imaging" surface — which had
sprawled into five competing entry points and three rival schedulers — was
**rebuilt under one roof**.

## What's verified

Skotiq is verified on **Windows**, where the desktop app drives ASCOM/Alpaca
cameras, focuser, filter wheel, and PHD2, with plate-solving and planning. The new
**master/slave live mirroring** (a Windows host serving its rig to a desktop
companion over the LAN) has been exercised end-to-end on the **local development
stack**; remote **monitoring and planning** from the web dashboard and Android
companion remain verified. Fully-unattended **headless** acquisition on real ASCOM
hardware is **still being hardened** and should be supervised. **Linux** is in
active testing (it's the development workstation and the slave-mode client for this
release); **macOS** builds in CI with no hardware soak. Every other device path is
**capability-gated**.

The 5.0 Living Sky pillars, the live-mirroring sync, and the automation rebuild
are verified at **static-analysis + unit/widget/parity-tested** level — the full
suite runs green across all packages, including new atlas/transient persistence,
master/slave wire-contract, and scheduler-scoring parity coverage — on top of the
4.1 real-hardware baseline. The native Living-Sky imaging engine and the on-sky
behaviour of the new automation paths were **not** re-run on a physical rig. The
on-sky caveat at the bottom matters this release.

## The Living Sky

Three pillars turn solving a frame into something that accumulates instead of
scrolling past.

- **Your Sky — a personal, growing atlas (Pillar A).** Every plate-solved frame
  folds into your own all-sky atlas. The more you image, the more of the sky you
  *own* — a coverage map that deepens region by region, night after night, instead
  of living as a folder of disconnected subs.
- **First Light — transient discovery (Pillar B).** Solved frames are scanned for
  things that weren't there before — new points of light and streaks — and surfaced
  as First Light detections you can review, dismiss, or chase. Your routine imaging
  quietly becomes a discovery survey.
- **Constellation — the community swarm (Pillar C).** Opt in to share, and the
  swarm's additive depth blends into Your Sky so faint structure shows up faster
  than any one rig could reach. Privacy is explicit and the contribution copy is
  honest about exactly what leaves your machine.

Backed by a new native imaging engine + bridge and additive schema migrations
(**v52–v55**) for atlas, transient, Constellation, and retention/index data.

## Your rig, mirrored on a second machine

5.0 adds **live master/slave mirroring**. One machine (the **master**, typically
the Windows laptop wired to the mount) owns the hardware and serves it; a second
machine (the **slave** — the desktop app launched with `--remote-host`) becomes a
live companion you can watch and drive. This path is contract- and local-stack
tested; a real second-machine LAN soak remains part of hardware validation.

- **Unified profiles and full state sync** — the slave sees the same equipment
  profiles, the same session, the same plan, updating in real time.
- **One coherent state, both ends.** As part of this release we audited every
  setting, toggle, and feature for master/slave parity and made **dozens of slave
  surfaces host-aware** — autopilot decisions, history, observing lists, notes,
  calibration libraries, the new atlas — so the slave reads the host's real data
  instead of an empty local store.
- **Smoother by design** — remote poll streams are de-duplicated, settings-mutation
  spam no longer thrashes the planner, and the idle slave stopped burning CPU on
  off-screen animation loops.

## One roof for automatic imaging

The app had quietly grown **five different places to start automated imaging** and
**three rival target-schedulers** with their own weights. 5.0 unifies them without
removing any capability:

- **One front door: "Plan Tonight."** Every Smart Night entrance now funnels
  through a single launcher with one consistent label.
- **Autopilot is its own clearly-named mode.** The continuous scheduler is now
  "Unattended Autopilot" — run hands-off and re-pick the best target all night —
  explicitly distinct from the plan-it-and-edit path. It also got **its own
  sequence slot**, so starting it no longer silently discards your unsaved manual
  sequence.
- **One source of truth under the hood.** The three target scorers now share one
  scoring contract (their numbers provably unchanged); safety config collapsed from
  three stores to one; the duplicated flat-calibration and focus-temperature
  engines merged; sidereal-time and mosaic-geometry math deduplicated. A read-only
  audit + plan drove the whole thing, executed in low-risk phases behind parity
  tests.

## Cloud backup, now to any S3-compatible store

Cloud backup used to speak only WebDAV (Nextcloud, ownCloud, and the like). It now
also speaks **S3**, so you can point your backups at Amazon S3, a self-hosted MinIO
server, or Backblaze B2. In **Settings → Backup & Sync** you pick the provider and
enter the endpoint, region, bucket, and keys (with a path-style toggle for MinIO);
**Test Connection** verifies your credentials and bucket before you save, and then
push/pull, auto-push, and restore all work exactly as they did with WebDAV. Your
secret key lives in the operating-system keyring, never in app settings or logs.
Existing WebDAV setups are untouched, and there's no database migration. The S3
path is covered by automated tests against an in-process object store; validating
it against your specific live S3/MinIO/B2 endpoint is recommended before you rely
on it.

## Navigation and performance

- A reorganized planner with **Discover / Framing / Planetarium** tabs and a
  cleaned-up shell navigation.
- **Per-screen title bars folded into the sub-tab row** across every sub-tabbed
  screen, reclaiming vertical space.
- The **Plan Tonight Recommendation tab never flashes** — it keeps the last good
  content during refresh instead of blanking, and heavy live-sky tabs preserve
  their state instead of rebuilding (via `TickerMode`), so it stopped pegging the
  CPU.

## Reliability and correctness

An adversarial audit (find → refute → fix) hardened the automation core. Notable
fixes:

- **A failed dispatch could wedge the autopilot** on a target it never started;
  it now commits a target only after dispatch succeeds and surfaces the error.
- **A Recovery node with a "Retry" action could abort an entire sequence load** —
  the Dart→Rust serialization didn't match the engine's expected shape. Fixed and
  pinned with cross-language tests.
- **B19:** loaded sequences no longer self-cancel at start — `MountTrackingLost`
  is edge-triggered instead of firing on the initial state.
- Benign sequencer lifecycle events stopped being mapped to errors; the slave
  stopped raising bogus critical alerts; a slave no longer tries to auto-create a
  sequence it isn't allowed to edit; `--serve` no longer hangs before `runApp`
  (blank window); scheduler stop/dispose stopped leaking subscriptions/streams.
- Three v5 Constellation features that *looked* done but were no-ops (a privacy
  toggle, the "blend into Your Sky" pull, an unreachable retract promise) were made
  real or made honest.

Constellation is **self-hosted** in 5.0. There is no Skotiq-operated public
hub or default account service: an operator runs `server/skotiq_hub` and
users enter that hub's URL. The hub now has its own analyze/test CI gate.

## Polish and accessibility

An adversarial UI pass executed the objective defects: tooltips/semantics on
icon-only controls, overflow hardening on narrow phone widths, error states that no
longer dump raw exceptions, a wizard "Next" that stops looking enabled when it
can't proceed, design-token cleanup, and de-duplicated "Plan Tonight" affordances
so two never compete on the same screen.

## Migrations

Four additive migrations, schema **v52 through v55**, persist the personal
atlas, First Light, Constellation, and retention/index data. In addition to the
synthetic migration suite, the release probe migrates a database created by the
tagged v4.3.0 schema (v51) to v55, verifies its SHA-256 before copying, checks
SQLite integrity, confirms all current tables/default settings, and preserves
the representative equipment profile.

## Updating from 5.0.0

Official 5.0.0 artifacts are **manual-update-only**. They contain no updater
executable, trusted update public key, or configured update server. The OTA
implementation remains fail-closed for custom/operator builds, but the public
5.0.0 package does not advertise or enable it. Back up from Settings, download
the next release from GitHub, and replace the portable bundle.

## The on-sky caveat (read this before an unattended night)

The Living Sky pillars, the live-mirroring sync, and the automation rebuild are
internally consistent and fully green under static analysis and the test suite —
scorer parity round-trips to the bit, the master/slave wire contract is enforced by
tests, and calibration/scoring behaviour is snapshot-pinned so it provably didn't
change — but the **native Living-Sky imaging engine and the new automation paths
have not been validated on a real rig under the stars**. A few behaviours can only
be proven with a real night: the sky-flat exposure fallback, the autopilot's
stop→restore-your-sequence ordering on live hardware, and the full Dart↔Rust
scorer-weight unification (contract-pinned here, not yet runtime-unified). Run a
supervised night before trusting an unattended one. Fully-unattended headless runs
should still be supervised; iOS and live cellular-push delivery still need a
Mac/Apple Developer account and your own Firebase/APNs keys.
