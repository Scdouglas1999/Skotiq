# Skotiq 3.0.0

Version 3.0.0 (build 13) · beta channel

This is a large release. The most visible change is a rebuilt Dashboard that now works as a live view of an imaging session, but there's a lot underneath it: a plate-solving path that actually works, a reorganized Settings, smarter planning, a tidier imaging screen, planetarium improvements, a mobile companion rebuilt to run your whole rig from your phone, and a long list of smaller fixes. The notes below walk through what changed.

## The Dashboard, rebuilt as a live session view

The Dashboard used to be a static readiness page. It's now the screen you can leave up for the whole night and read your session from.

It stays quiet until there's something to show. With nothing connected, it presents a standby view — a button to plan tonight, a summary of your last run, and a short readiness check of your gear. As soon as you connect equipment or load a sequence, it fills in with live data.

When a session is running you get, in one place: a compact status line with the current target, its altitude, time to the meridian, the running exposure's countdown, and overall sequence progress with an estimate of when it'll finish; the latest sub with a strip of recent frames beneath it; live equipment telemetry (cooling, mount, focuser, filter wheel, rotator, guider); the guiding trace and RMS; weather and safety; and a feed of your sequence's triggers as they fire.

You can drive the run from here too — start, pause, resume, skip, and stop — with stop deliberately requiring a press-and-hold so a stray click can't end a long run. Critical events and recovery state pin to the top so they can't scroll away, and those events now reach your notifications as well.

The layout is yours to arrange: drag panels around, move them between columns, resize them, and hide what you don't want. A handful of optional panels — target scheduler, cloud motion, an adaptive-conditions score, a live light curve for photometry runs — are off by default. Existing layouts are migrated automatically on upgrade.

Because all of this lives on the Dashboard now, the Sequencer's old "Run" tab has been removed. The Sequencer is where you build a sequence; the Dashboard is where you run and watch it.

## Planning a night

The "Plan Tonight" assistant builds a full plan from your equipment and the sky in a short wizard. It works out tonight's dark window from your location, confirms your gear, picks targets by score (or lets you choose your own), suggests a strategy that matches the filters you actually have — LRGB, narrowband HOO or SHO, one-shot color — and shows a full timeline and per-filter breakdown you can adjust before accepting. It accounts for light pollution, cloud probability, the age of your last polar alignment, and gaps in your dark library, and it can schedule the missing calibration for you.

Planning now extends beyond a single night. Multi-night projects pair with an Open-Meteo forecast that highlights the best nights in the coming week, so you can line a target up against the weather instead of guessing.

The Planner is the one place for tonight's recommendations — a scored, filterable list with altitude curves and integration estimates. Every target in it can now go straight to the Sequencer (previously only the top pick could), to Framing, or to an observing list, and you can search past your local catalog with SIMBAD name resolution. The duplicate "Suggestions" tab that used to live in Framing has been removed.

## Plate solving

Plate solving with ASTAP has been reworked and is dependable again. It now uses the ASTAP binary and catalog folder you actually configured instead of auto-detecting something else; it writes its result to a sidecar file rather than modifying your light frame, which the previous version could do; it passes ASTAP its catalog directory and reads the real success-or-failure result instead of assuming; and it enforces the solve timeout so a stuck solve can't stall a sequence. If centering or sync had been failing — or sequences wouldn't start because the solve failed instantly — this should clear it up. If you'd set a custom ASTAP path before, it's worth re-confirming it under Settings → Plate Solving.

## Imaging and capture

The imaging screen reads more like a live capture now. Frames carry a quick quality badge, there's a guiding indicator, and a meridian-flip countdown you can dismiss. Color-camera users get a real one-shot-color path — debayer, register on luminance, then integrate per channel — alongside live stacking and a one-button stack-and-share. The on-image controls were grouped together, set to hide when idle, and pulled off the frame so the image isn't buried under buttons. Annotations now report an error when a catalog lookup fails instead of quietly showing nothing, and the camera cooler no longer starts the moment you connect.

## Planetarium

The sky chart gained field-of-view rings that draw your equipment's frame over a target, and a red night-vision mode for dark-adapted planning. Rendering is considerably smoother at higher detail after a performance pass — the static and animated layers were separated so twinkle and motion no longer force the whole catalog to redraw — and the overall look is cleaner and more legible.

## Settings

The flat list of thirty-odd pages is gone. Settings is now seven collapsible groups with a search box at the top, so you can type "latitude," "ASTAP," or "flats" and go straight there. Three single-purpose pages were folded into sensible homes — Files & Storage, Autofocus (now including predictive autofocus), and Notifications (now including delivery routing). Existing in-app links to specific settings still land where they did.

## Equipment and onboarding

The interface follows what your hardware reports rather than a generic superset: cooler, rotator, pulse-guide, read mode, binning, and tracking rate all reflect the connected device. Onboarding reads your filter wheel's real slot count and names instead of assuming five. First launch is a single guided walkthrough rather than several scattered prompts, with follow-up nudges toward framing, focusing, and first light. Device discovery is event-driven with a periodic fallback and a manual rescan button, and filter-wheel and focuser connections hold up better under load.

## Framing

Framing was rebuilt around an astrometrically correct workflow. A guided framing assistant registers the survey image to real sky coordinates, so what you frame is what you'll actually capture; it accounts for camera and field rotation, and caches survey imagery so you can frame offline. The view itself uses GPU-accelerated HiPS survey tiles — zoomable, registered real-sky imagery that stays smooth as you pan and zoom — and a mosaic you lay out here carries through to the sequencer with your composition and rotation intact.

## Weather, safety, and automation

There's a live cloud-motion readout that estimates when clouds will arrive or clear, adaptive conditions that can adjust a plan as the sky changes, and safety watchdogs wired into the Plan Tonight flow. A round of fixes went specifically into unattended overnight runs to make all-night sessions more dependable.

## Mobile companion

The companion app was rebuilt around a simple idea: paired with the desktop that's wired to your rig, your phone should let you run the whole session as if the gear were in your hand — and it should feel native on the phone, not like a shrunken desktop.

The interface now adapts to whatever screen it's on. Every screen reflows for the phone in both portrait and landscape — including unusual shapes like a foldable's cover display — and the size of things scales to the space instead of carrying desktop proportions onto a small screen. The navigation and status bars tuck away on demand, or after a moment of inactivity, so the content gets the whole screen, with a grab handle to bring them back. You can tap the live image to inspect it full-screen with pinch-to-zoom, and connection state moved to a small ambient indicator instead of a bar across the top.

Over the network the phone drives the rig directly: connect and disconnect equipment, change camera settings and start a snapshot or a loop, move the focuser, filter wheel, and rotator, run, pause, resume, and stop a sequence and watch its progress, start and calibrate guiding and read the guide graph, run autofocus, plate-solve and center on a target, frame a target and send it to the sequencer, and slew the mount from the sky chart. The live preview streams to the phone as the host captures; you can re-stretch a frame, read its histogram and statistics, see a debayered color preview, and download the full frame to your phone. Setup that used to assume local hardware now operates on the host where the gear actually is — configuring and verifying your plate solver, rescanning for devices, and the live sub on the Dashboard all reach across to the desktop rather than looking at the phone.

It connects over Tailscale, directly across your tailnet with no relay in between, and recovers its connection cleanly after a drop.

## Other fixes and polish

Sequence nodes show a short summary with inline quick-edit, so you can adjust a node's main settings without opening its full panel. There's a new Integrations page, inline help on form fields, quieter telemetry and logs, smoother cooler readouts, faster discovery, and a long tail of stability fixes across sequencing, equipment, framing, and the database. The visual style was tightened up as well.

## Under the hood

This release also carries the largest internal cleanup the project has had — a broad refactor that breaks its biggest, most tangled files into smaller, tested pieces. It's invisible from the app, but it makes things more stable now and easier to build on going forward.

## Upgrading

This is a beta build. Your data carries over as-is — sequences, profiles, targets, and capture history — with nothing to do by hand. Your Dashboard layout migrates to the new version automatically; if you'd rather start from the default, reset it from the customize menu. Runs are started and monitored from the Dashboard now rather than the Sequencer. And if you use plate solving, re-confirm your ASTAP path under Settings → Plate Solving.
