# Skotiq 5.0.0

Version 5.0.0 (build 23)

This is a big one — nearly 100 changes since 4.3.0. The headline is
**Living Sky**: a personal, growing atlas of everything you've imaged, a "First
Light" path that turns a night's captures into real scientific contributions, and
a Constellation mode that lets more than one rig work the same target together.
But there's a lot more underneath: **live master/slave mirroring** so you can run
your whole rig from another machine, a unified **automation core**, a reorganized
set of screens, **S3-compatible cloud backup**, and a deep layer of security,
safety, and reliability hardening. The notes below walk through the major changes,
and a categorized change summary follows at the end.

## Living Sky

Living Sky is built on a new native imaging engine and bridge, with new database
storage for the atlas and your transient/discovery history. It comes in three
pillars.

### Your Sky — an atlas that grows as you image

Your Sky is a personal atlas. Every target you capture is folded into it
automatically when a frame solves, and the map fills in over time as a record of
where you've actually pointed and what you collected. You can name regions, watch
coverage grow, browse real pixel crops of what you've shot, and see your whole sky
at a glance on an all-sky map.

### First Light — from a capture to a real contribution

First Light scans solved frames for transients, variable stars, and minor planets,
and turns a detection into something you can review and report. Skotiq can
submit a confirmed transient through the real TNS bot API when you configure
credentials. AAVSO and MPC output is generated as standards-shaped files for you
to review and upload manually; Skotiq does not submit those files for you. A
"Chase" flow helps you re-observe a target, and there's a live light
curve for photometry runs. The emphasis throughout is honesty: the app only
presents a measurement as science when it actually is one, and shows its working
rather than a confident-looking number it can't stand behind.

### Constellation — more than one rig on the same sky

Constellation lets a set of rigs cooperate through the included self-hosted hub.
Rigs advertise their reachability, claim and cleanly retract regions, and share
additive atlas-tile contributions so the group's data lands in one place rather
than siloed per machine. There is no Skotiq-operated public hub or default
account service in 5.0: an operator runs `server/skotiq_hub`, and users enter
that hub's URL. The hub is now analyzed and tested in CI.

A dedicated ship-readiness pass (116 reviewed findings across several waves) went
into making these pillars actually trustworthy before release — stopping atlas and
Constellation data corruption, tightening the science-honesty story, and proving
master/slave parity with tests.

## Run your rig from another machine — live master/slave mirroring

Skotiq can now run as a **live master/slave pair**. One machine acts as the
master running the session; another runs in **desktop slave mode** and mirrors it —
unified equipment profiles, full state sync, and even the host's autopilot
decisions reflected on the slave. A focused triage campaign made 29 separate
surfaces remote-aware and closed the sync gaps found in a coverage audit, and a
round of "de-jank" work stopped the slave from throwing bogus critical errors,
thrashing profiles, or burning idle CPU on animation loops. The result is that you
can watch and drive a running rig from a second computer and see a faithful copy of
what's happening.

## A more coherent automation core

The automatic-imaging systems were unified behind a single, consistent core. There
is now one "Plan Tonight" front door instead of several overlapping entry points;
safety configuration, local-sidereal-time math, and mosaic geometry are
single-sourced rather than reimplemented in different places; the flat-calibration
and focus-temperature models were merged behind a parity gate; and autopilot now
shares one DECIDE scoring contract and owns its own scheduling slot. This is mostly
invisible, but it makes planning, scheduling, and autopilot behave consistently no
matter where you start from.

## Screens and navigation, reorganized

The app's navigation was reworked: a new **Discover** tab in the planner, a cleaner
shell navigation, and reorganized science and analytics areas. Sub-tabbed screens
now carry a consistent title bar, and the "Plan Tonight" Recommendation tab no
longer flashes as you move around. The heavy live-sky tabs (Framing, Planetarium,
Discover) build lazily and preserve their state when you switch away, so navigation
stays responsive. Two adversarial UI review passes cleaned up objective defects
plus accessibility, overflow, and design-token issues.

## Cloud backup: now any S3-compatible store

Cloud backup previously spoke only WebDAV (Nextcloud, ownCloud, and similar). It
now also speaks **S3**, so you can point your backups at Amazon S3, a self-hosted
MinIO server, or Backblaze B2.

In **Settings → Backup & Sync**, pick your provider, enter the endpoint, region,
bucket, access key, and secret key. There's a path-style toggle for MinIO and other
self-hosted gateways. Use **Test Connection** to confirm your credentials and bucket
before saving, then backup push/pull, auto-push, and restore all work exactly as
they did with WebDAV. Your secret key is stored in the operating system's keyring,
never in app settings or logs. Existing WebDAV setups are untouched.

## Security, safety, and reliability

A large share of this release went into hardening the parts of Skotiq you don't
see but rely on:

- **Remote control is harder to abuse.** The headless server ties rate-limiting and
  lockout to the real network connection rather than a forgeable header, checks
  authentication before it buffers a request body, throttles pairing and websocket
  attempts, and tightens file-path validation (including symlink-ancestor
  resolution) so the file endpoints can't be coaxed into probing paths outside the
  folders you've allowed.
- **Updates fail closed.** The updater code requires a valid Ed25519 signature and
  re-verifies it at install time. Official 5.0.0 artifacts deliberately ship with
  no updater executable, trusted public key, or update server, so 5.0 is
  manual-update-only. Operator-built OTA packages remain disabled unless all three
  pieces are provisioned.
- **Your gear won't be double-commanded.** Non-idempotent hardware commands — a
  relative focuser/rotator move, a mount slew, an exposure — are sent exactly once
  even over a slow or flaky remote link.
- **Sessions clean up after themselves.** The sequencer reliably reaps timed-out
  child processes and their process groups; loaded sequences no longer self-cancel
  at start from an edge case in tracking-loss handling; and a number of leaked
  images, controllers, and subscriptions were fixed.
- **Better error reporting.** Errors over the remote API now carry a stable
  machine-readable code alongside the human message, in a backward-compatible
  envelope.

## Imaging correctness and code quality

- **Imaging math.** Master-frame accumulation got overflow-checked geometry,
  corrected cross-fold frame weighting, and a more robust online clipping-variance
  path — improvements to the math behind your stacked masters, with the on-disk
  format unchanged.
- **Device hot-plug** events were promoted to first-class events end to end, so the
  app reacts more cleanly when gear is connected or removed mid-session.
- **A quieter, tighter codebase.** Broad cleanup removed proven-dead code and
  duplicate paths, consolidated logic that had drifted apart, replaced weak or
  self-referential tests with ones that assert real behavior, and added new
  consistency and boundary gates to keep it that way. A correctness/quality audit
  pass fixed nine confirmed findings.

## Platform and packaging

- Official Windows archives bundle the Skotiq bridge, LibRaw, and MSVC runtime;
  they do not redistribute vendor device SDKs. Install vendor drivers/SDKs yourself
  or use ASCOM/Alpaca.
- The Linux release artifact matches the runnable bundle layout.
- A `--serve` startup hang (blank window) was fixed, and a Windows CI runner was
  pinned to fix a flaky symlink-extraction failure.
- Versions were reconciled across the workspace to 5.0.0 and are guarded by a
  consistency check.
- Both desktop archives contain the Skotiq license, current third-party
  notices, and the LGPL-2.1 license used for dynamically linked LibRaw.
- The Windows portable beta is not code-signed and may trigger SmartScreen; the
  Android APK is a debug-signed sideload build.

## Upgrade notes and compatibility

- **Your configuration is preserved.** Saved profiles, sequences, cloud-sync
  setups, and layouts carry forward.
- **Database migration.** Additive schema versions 52–55 add atlas, discovery,
  Constellation, retention, and index data. A release-authentic v4.3.0 schema-51
  database is migrated to v55 in the release gate with integrity, table, default,
  hash, and representative-profile preservation checks.
- **Update manually.** Back up, download the next portable bundle from GitHub, and
  replace the existing bundle. Official 5.0.0 artifacts do not enable OTA updates.
- **Known limitations** are tracked in `docs/known-limitations.md`. In particular,
  the new S3 cloud-backup path is verified in automated tests against an in-process
  mock object store; validate it against your specific live S3, MinIO, or Backblaze
  B2 endpoint before relying on it.
